Background. Reliability of automated radiological analysis is limited less by model accuracy than by having no mechanism to refuse. A pre-trained segmentation network returns a measurement for any image, including acquisitions outside its validated range, and when analysis is delegated to agents nothing is obliged to notice. Methods. In The Cancer Imaging Archive we identified 193 reconstruction pairs in 59 patients: two CT series from one irradiation event, matched on DICOM frame of reference and acquisition time, differing only in convolution kernel or slice thickness. TotalSegmentator 2.18.0 measured heart, aorta, pulmonary vein and venae cavae across 217 series. Three endpoints and a 10% threshold were fixed in advance as assertions. Results. An eligibility check halted one endpoint at five patients, on metadata. Reconstructions of one acquisition need not share z-coverage: median 15%, and 59% of pairs exceeded 5% despite matched frame of reference; coverage predicts volume difference (rho 0.70), confounding any study pairing series on those keys. In 45 coverage-matched pairs of identical thickness, kernel change altered heart volume by 0.63% (95% CI 0.50-0.97) and pulmonary vein by 4.96% (3.20-5.63); all intervals excluded 10%. Segmentation failed on 4 of 217 series. Radiogenomic association discordance, volumes against tumor RNA-seq in the same patients, did not exceed a permutation null (p=0.33). Conclusions. Whole-heart volumetry on chest CT is reproducible across kernels to about 1% when field of view matches; thin vessels vary several times more. The failed assumption was prose, where it could not halt; measurement caught it.
