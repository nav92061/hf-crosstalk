# Heart failure and cancer: what the literature establishes, and what it does not

## Introduction

### The experimental case that a failing heart can promote tumour growth

The claim that heart failure is not merely a comorbidity of cancer but a condition that
actively promotes it rests on a small number of animal experiments, of which one is
foundational. Meijers and colleagues induced heart failure by large anterior myocardial
infarction in APC-mutant mice predisposed to intestinal tumours and found a 2.4-fold
increase in intestinal tumour load; critically, they repeated the experiment using
heterotopic transplantation of an infarcted heart into a recipient whose own heart was left
in situ, which reproduced the tumour-promoting effect while excluding haemodynamic
impairment as the cause, and nominated serpinA3, serpinA1, fibronectin and ceruloplasmin as
candidate circulating mediators (Meijers 2018, PMID: 29459363). That design is the reason
this single paper carries so much weight: it converts a correlation into a claim about
something transported in blood. It was contested in print, with two Letters and a published
Response appearing in the same journal the following year (Meijers 2019, PMID: 30689420).

Three subsequent studies converge on the same direction through different mechanisms.
Avraham and colleagues showed that pressure-overload cardiac remodelling by transverse
aortic constriction, in the absence of overt heart failure, was sufficient to produce larger
primary tumours and more metastases; serum from these mice promoted cancer cell
proliferation in vitro, and depleting periostin from that serum abolished the effect while
adding recombinant periostin restored it (Avraham 2020, PMID: 32475164). Koelwyn and
colleagues found that myocardial infarction accelerated breast cancer outgrowth by
epigenetically reprogramming Ly6C-high bone marrow monocytes toward an immunosuppressive
phenotype, with monocyte depletion abrogating the effect (Koelwyn 2020, PMID: 32661390).
Tani and colleagues traced post-infarction mammary tumour growth to elevated circulating
nerve growth factor acting on TRKA, and showed that a TRKA inhibitor suppressed tumour
volume in infarcted mice (Tani 2023, PMID: 38510296). A fourth mechanism, cardiac small
extracellular vesicles, produced tumour promotion that differed by cancer type, with lung
and colon lines responding more than melanoma and breast lines (Caller 2024, PMID:
38487879).

Two features of this literature deserve emphasis before it is used to justify anything. The
first is that the mechanisms are not the same mechanism: a secreted matricellular protein,
a reprogrammed monocyte compartment, a neurotrophin-receptor kinase axis and a vesicle
cargo are four different explanations, and no study has established which dominates. The
second is that the secreted-factor explanation is already known to be non-exclusive within
its own line of work. When periostin was genetically deleted, cardiac-remodelling-dependent
tumour promotion persisted, and it was integrin beta-1 loss of function in the cancer cells
that abolished it (Langier Goncalves 2023, PMID: 38139195). Periostin is therefore
sufficient but not necessary, and the operative signal is better described as a set of
integrin-binding ligands than as any single protein.

The reciprocal direction complicates the picture further. In the same experimental system,
tumour-bearing mice subjected to pressure overload developed less cardiac hypertrophy, less
fibrosis and better contractile function than tumour-free controls, an effect mediated by
tumour-driven M1-to-M2 polarisation of cardiac macrophages (Awwad 2023, PMID: 37508517).
Whatever passes between heart and tumour is bidirectional and not uniformly deleterious.

### The epidemiology is consistent in sign and unreliable in magnitude

Observational cohorts have repeatedly found more cancer among patients with heart failure.
The Olmsted County community cohort reported a 68% higher adjusted risk of incident cancer
(HR 1.68, 95% CI 1.13-2.50) with adjustment for body mass index, smoking and comorbidities,
and importantly found no excess of prior cancer history at the index date (Hasin 2013, PMID:
23810869). A Danish cohort of 9,307 chronic heart failure outpatients found an incidence
rate ratio of 1.24 (95% CI 1.15-1.33) versus the general population, elevated in every age
stratum and for all major cancer types except prostate (Banke 2016, PMID: 26751260). A
German study matching 100,124 heart failure patients to controls on sex, age, diabetes,
obesity and consultation frequency reported cancer in 25.7% versus 16.2% over ten years
(HR 1.76, 95% CI 1.71-1.81) (Roderburg 2021, PMID: 34180146). A community cohort from Puglia
matching 104,020 pairs found both higher cancer incidence (HR 1.76) and much higher cancer
mortality (HR 4.11; competing-risk subdistribution HR 3.48) (Bertero 2022, PMID: 35492831).
Pooled across four cohorts totalling five million participants, the relative risk was 1.22
(95% CI 1.13-1.33) (Zhang 2020, PMID: 31745841).

The strongest single counter-observation is not a null in a small study but a null in the
largest and best-adjudicated one. In 28,341 Physicians' Health Study participants followed
for a median of 19.9 years, with annual health evaluations and adjudicated cancer diagnoses,
heart failure modelled as a time-varying exposure was not associated with cancer incidence
(adjusted HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.86-1.29) or with cancer-specific mortality (Selvaraj 2018,
PMID: 29622155). A paper that intends to build on the positive cohorts must engage this
result rather than list it.

### The confounding problem

Three distinct biases operate here and they are not interchangeable.

Surveillance and detection bias is the most tractable, and one study has quantified it
directly. In a Korean national cohort of 128,441 heart failure patients the crude cancer
hazard ratio was 1.64 (95% CI 1.61-1.68), but excluding cancers diagnosed within two years
of the heart failure diagnosis attenuated the association to HR 1.09 (95% CI 1.05-1.13) -
that is, roughly five sixths of the apparent excess disappeared with a two-year lag, and
only lung, hepatobiliary/pancreatic and haematologic cancers remained consistently elevated
(Kwak 2020, PMID: 32863081). Any claim about the magnitude of a heart-failure-to-cancer
effect that does not report a lagged estimate is reporting mostly detection.

Shared risk factors are the least tractable, because they generate the association without
any causal arrow between the two diseases at all. The Framingham risk score, constructed
entirely from conventional cardiovascular risk factors, predicted both incident cancer
(subdistribution HR 2.32) and incident heart failure (sHR 10.08) in PREVEND, with
replication in 389,942 UK Biobank participants (van den Berg 2026, PMID: 41014601). Chronic
low-grade inflammation behaves the same way: baseline high-sensitivity C-reactive protein
predicted incident heart failure or cancer in HUNT3 (HR 1.09) and Health ABC (HR 1.11), as
did IL-1, IL-6 and TNF-alpha (Bertero 2024, PMID: 38429011). A shared antecedent that
predicts both diseases is precisely the pattern a causal HF-to-cancer effect would also
produce, and observational data cannot separate them.

Reverse causation and disease sequencing form a third problem that is often overlooked.
In UK Biobank, comorbidities accumulated for more than a decade before the heart failure
diagnosis and accelerated in the years immediately preceding it, with cancer among the
diseases most commonly diagnosed just before heart failure (MacGowan 2025, PMID: 39935109).
Heart failure is typically the fourth diagnosis in a multimorbidity trajectory, not a clean
baseline exposure. Treatment is a further confounder: within the Puglia cohort, high-dose
loop diuretic prescription was itself associated with higher cancer incidence and mortality
(Bertero 2022, PMID: 35492831), and spironolactone, prescribed to a large fraction of heart
failure patients, carries a regulatory tumorigenicity warning that has been the subject of
its own meta-analysis (Bommareddy 2022, PMID: 35138351). Competing risk of death is
formally handled in only some of these analyses; the Puglia study is notable for reporting
a competing-risk subdistribution hazard, and van den Berg and colleagues used Fine-Gray
models with mortality as the competing event.

### The shared-substrate alternative

Clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential provides the clearest worked example of a
mechanism that produces both cardiovascular disease and cancer without either causing the
other. CHIP carriers have roughly double the risk of coronary heart disease and four-fold
risk of early-onset myocardial infarction, and Tet2 perturbation in atherosclerosis-prone
mice supported causality (Jaiswal 2017, PMID: 28636844); TET2-deficient clonal
haematopoiesis increased plaque size with augmented NLRP3-mediated IL-1beta secretion
(Fuster 2017, PMID: 28104796). The same somatic clones carry increased risk of
haematological malignancy, and high rates of CHIP are found in patients with solid tumours
(Reed 2023, PMID: 36454121). In heart failure specifically, DNMT3A or TET2 mutations
predicted accelerated progression (Pascual-Figal 2021, PMID: 33832602), TET2-mediated clonal
haematopoiesis was enriched in HFpEF versus controls (Cochran 2023, PMID: 37681311), and a
meta-analysis of 57,755 participants found clonal haematopoiesis associated with incident
heart failure (HR 1.23, 95% CI 1.12-1.35) (Karakasis 2025, PMID: 40064512). Notably, most
CHIP carriers never develop blood cancer (Evans 2022, PMID: 36049115), so even this
mechanism is a risk-modifier rather than a determinant.

The one piece of randomised human evidence that an inflammatory pathway shared with
cardiovascular disease is causal for cancer comes from CANTOS, where IL-1beta inhibition
with canakinumab reduced incident lung cancer in patients with atherosclerosis and elevated
hsCRP (Ridker 2017, PMID: 28855077). This is the strongest causal anchor in the entire
shared-substrate literature, and it is worth being precise about what it shows: that
inhibiting an inflammatory mediator reduces cancer incidence in people with atherosclerosis.
It does not show that heart failure causes cancer.

## Why a ligand-receptor compatibility design is reasonable, and where it stops

The design is defensible for one specific reason: the founding experiment in this field
was itself a secreted-factor experiment, and the one ligand it has yielded that survives
independent replication - periostin - was identified by depleting it from heart-failure serum
and watching the tumour-promoting activity disappear (Avraham 2020, PMID: 32475164).
Periostin acts on integrins, its tumour biology through integrin beta-3/beta-5-ERK-NF-kB
signalling is established in ovarian cancer (Lin 2022, PMID: 36550569) and in
periostin-high cancer-associated fibroblasts in cervical carcinoma (Wei 2020, PMID:
33124726), and the requirement for integrin beta-1 on the cancer cell was demonstrated
genetically in the cardiac model (Langier Goncalves 2023, PMID: 38139195). A framework that
pairs a heart-failure-upregulated secreted ligand with the expression of its receptor in a
given tumour lineage is therefore asking the same question the successful experiments asked,
and it offers a principled reason why susceptibility might differ by cancer type: a
circulating ligand can only act where its receptor is present. The observation that cardiac
extracellular vesicles promoted lung and colon tumours more than melanoma and breast
(Caller 2024, PMID: 38487879) is exactly the kind of tissue-selective effect such a
framework predicts.

Four limitations bound what the design can conclude, and each is documented rather than
hypothetical.

Transcript abundance is not secreted protein abundance, and in this literature the
dissociation has been measured. Plasma galectin-3 did not correlate with myocardial
galectin-3 expression or with left ventricular fibrosis in patients with endomyocardial
biopsies (Besler 2017, PMID: 28288987). Similarly, the dominant circulating form of BNP in
human heart failure is the weakly active proBNP rather than mature BNP (Matsuo 2018, PMID:
30120963), so NPPB transcript elevation overstates the bioactive signal. THBS4 has an
intracellular endoplasmic-reticulum-stress function in addition to being secreted, so
transcript up does not establish secretion up.

Cardiac expression is not cardiac endocrine action. The cleanest demonstration is CCN2:
cell-specific deletion showed that cardiomyocyte-derived CCN2 is dispensable for cardiac
fibrosis, that fibroblast-derived CCN2 is the functional pool, and that CCN2 acts in an
autocrine manner despite being secreted (Dorn 2018, PMID: 30040954). A ligand can be
strongly induced in the failing heart and still never act at a distance.

Receptor expression is not receptor engagement, and the direction of effect is not
inferable from expression. GDF15 is among the best-established heart-failure-elevated
circulating proteins (Wollert 2016, PMID: 28062617; Ceelen 2022, PMID: 34989084), but its
canonical receptor GFRAL is restricted to the area postrema and nucleus tractus solitarius
of the brainstem (Hsu 2017, PMID: 28953886), so circulating GDF15 cannot signal to a
peripheral tumour through it at all; its established cancer role is host cachexia
(Suriben 2020, PMID: 32661391). GDF15 is a useful negative control for a
compatibility-scoring design precisely because it would score highly on ligand evidence and
must score zero on receptor accessibility. The sign problem is equally real: secreted
frizzled-related proteins are Wnt antagonists whose genes are silenced by hypermethylation
across cancers (Surana 2013, PMID: 24316024; Suzuki 2008, PMID: 18283316), so more SFRP
ligand predicts less Wnt-driven tumour growth, and a compatibility score would carry the
wrong sign unless antagonism is modelled explicitly.

Finally, expression-based ligand-receptor inference is a known-imperfect method. The field
has produced over a hundred tools and nearly fifty ligand-receptor resources with widely
differing assumptions, scoring approaches and limitations (Cesaro 2025, PMID: 40536815),
and the canonical implementations are explicit that they infer rather than measure
communication (Jin 2021, PMID: 33597522; Efremova 2020, PMID: 32103204). For scale, a
proteomic analysis of 1,459 proteins in 44,313 UK Biobank participants found 820
protein-disease associations across cardiac diseases but Mendelian randomisation supported
causal roles for only about 4% of them (Schuermans 2024, PMID: 39572695). The appropriate
output of a compatibility analysis is a ranked hypothesis list, not an effect estimate.

## Discussion: the candidate axes and how much each can bear

**Periostin-integrin is the only axis with an unbroken chain of evidence.** Periostin mRNA
is increased in explanted human failing myocardium and its protein, undetectable in control
hearts, correlates with fibrosis and left ventricular diastolic dimension (Zhao 2013, PMID:
24219836); single-cell profiling of 45 human hearts localises it to a FAP/POSTN
disease-associated matrifibrocyte state arising through macrophage IL-1beta signalling
(Amrute 2024, PMID: 39443792); serum depletion establishes functional necessity in the mouse
model (Avraham 2020, PMID: 32475164); and its integrin receptors have documented
pro-metastatic function in gynaecologic cancers (Lin 2022, PMID: 36550569; Wei 2020, PMID:
33124726). The caveat is that periostin is not required for the phenotype (Langier Goncalves
2023, PMID: 38139195).

**FSTL1-DIP2A/TLR4 and THBS4-integrin alpha-2 are the best-supported novel candidates, both
with direction problems.** Serum FSTL1 is 56% above matched controls in systolic heart
failure with a 2.5-fold increase in explanted failing myocardium (El-Armouche 2011, PMID:
21622850), and in cancer, fibroblast-secreted FSTL1 promotes hepatocellular carcinoma via
TLR4-AKT/mTOR (Loh 2021, PMID: 34551961) while DIP2A on tumour cells is required for
FSTL1-driven immunoresistance (Kudo-Saito 2018, PMID: 30110636). But cardiomyocyte-specific
Fstl1 deletion worsens HFpEF (Tanaka 2016, PMID: 27430031), so cardiac FSTL1 is locally
protective while the same protein is peripherally pro-tumorigenic - elevation in heart
failure may be compensatory. THBS4 rises markedly in hypertrophic and failing hearts
(Frolova 2012, PMID: 22362893) and CAF-derived TSP-4 signals through integrin alpha-2 to
HSF1 in gallbladder cancer (Shi 2021, PMID: 33407730), but in cancer THBS4 is
characteristically a stromal response protein induced locally (McCart Reed 2013, PMID:
23942617), which weakens the argument that a distant cardiac source adds signal.

**The opioid axis is the axis most likely to be nominated by a transcript analysis and least
supported by the literature.** Plasma proenkephalin is elevated in acute heart failure and
HFpEF and independently prognostic (Ng 2017, PMID: 28057251), but its strongest independent
predictor is estimated glomerular filtration rate and it clusters with renal markers, so
elevated PENK in heart failure is substantially a cardiorenal readout rather than evidence
of cardiac secretion (Emmens 2019, PMID: 31091993). On the receptor side, the largest
analysis - 8,780 tumour and normal samples across twelve solid tumour types from TCGA and
GTEx - found OPRK1 and OPRD1 transcripts enriched in tumour versus normal tissue (odds
ratios 1.27 and 1.66) and OPRM1 depleted (0.74), but no association between tumour opioid
receptor expression and disease-free interval or overall survival after adjustment
(Belltall 2022, PMID: 35359418). Where kappa-opioid pharmacology has been tested, the
direction is anti-tumour: KOR agonists suppressed angiogenesis via VEGFR2 and KOR-knockout
mice grew larger tumours (Yamamizu 2013, PMID: 24225480). Two randomised trials of
opioid-sparing anaesthesia found no reduction in cancer recurrence (Sessler 2019, PMID:
31645288; Xu 2021, PMID: 34192298). The reproducible enkephalin growth effect runs through
OGFR, a nuclear-envelope protein with no structural homology to the classical opioid
receptors (Zagon 2002, PMID: 11890982; Donahue 2011, PMID: 21531450). A nominated
PENK-OPRK1 axis should therefore be presented as a transcript-level coincidence requiring
validation, not as a mechanism - and the two most-cited papers for delta-opioid-driven
hepatocellular progression are retracted.

**FZD2 in gynaecologic cancer is a real gap.** FZD2 drives EMT and metastasis through a
noncanonical Fyn-STAT3 pathway with antibody proof-of-concept in xenografts (Gujral 2014,
PMID: 25417160), predicts poor prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (Ou 2019, PMID:
30677195) and breast cancer (Tuluhong 2021, PMID: 33832493), and is upregulated pan-cancer
(Zhou 2025, PMID: 40444048). For ovarian and endometrial cancer specifically, targeted
searching returned no study measuring FZD2 as a primary endpoint. The supporting evidence is
indirect: WNT5A is upregulated across all major epithelial ovarian cancer subtypes in 623
patients (Ford 2014, PMID: 24924122), host-derived WNT5A from peritoneal mesothelium and
visceral adipose drives peritoneal metastasis (Asem 2020, PMID: 31932454), and WNT5A
overexpression upregulates FZD2 and FZD5 in ovarian lines with a FZD5 antagonist abolishing
the phenotype (Azimian-Zavareh 2021, PMID: 33723319). If the analysis nominates a
FZD2-gynaecologic axis, the paper must say that FZD2 itself is unvalidated in these tumours.

**Natriuretic peptides and SERPINA3 are weaker than their prominence suggests.** NPPA and
NPPB are the least ambiguous heart-failure-upregulated ligands in existence (Goetze 2020,
PMID: 32444692), but NPR1 is pro-tumorigenic in gastric cancer (Li 2016, PMID: 27634171;
Sasahara 2023, PMID: 38151776) while ANP-family peptides are reported to kill cancer cells
at micromolar concentrations, with BNP inactive even at 100-fold higher doses (Vesely 2005,
PMID: 16297362) - the direction of any predicted effect is unresolved. SERPINA3, one of the
original Meijers candidates and recurrent across failing-myocardium microarray datasets
(Asakura 2009, PMID: 19638707), has no defined single-pass receptor and cannot be scored in
a ligand-receptor framework at all; its cancer effect is tumour-suppressive in lung
(Jin 2023, PMID: 37417362) and tumour-promoting in triple-negative breast cancer
(Zhang 2021, PMID: 33569740).

**COMP points the wrong way.** COMP expression was decreased in dilated cardiomyopathy
patient hearts relative to donors, and COMP-knockout mice spontaneously developed dilated
cardiomyopathy (Huang 2013, PMID: 23917519), even though COMP is overexpressed in fifteen
human cancers (Guo 2022, PMID: 36255654). If a transcript analysis nominates COMP as
heart-failure-upregulated, that contradicts the published human myocardial direction and
requires explicit comment.

## Where the evidence is thin

The honest summary is that the mechanistic literature is four or five mouse experiments with
non-overlapping mechanisms, the epidemiology is consistent in sign but loses most of its
magnitude to a two-year diagnostic lag and is contradicted by the largest adjudicated
cohort, and the ligand-level evidence is transcript-and-plasma-biomarker evidence rather
than evidence of cardiac-to-tumour signalling. No study has demonstrated that a
heart-failure-derived circulating ligand engages its receptor on a human tumour. No human
interventional evidence exists that treating heart failure reduces cancer incidence. The
only randomised causal anchor in the adjacent literature (CANTOS) targets IL-1beta in
atherosclerosis, not heart failure. A hypothesis-generating design is appropriate to this
evidence base; an effect estimate is not.
